My analytical view

AI resembles the dot-com period in expectations, infrastructure investment and concentration. It differs because many leaders are established cash-generating companies with less extreme valuations. The critical risk is a mismatch between AI capex and sustainable cash returns, particularly for highly leveraged infrastructure providers.

Most plausible failure mode: an investment-cycle slowdown and earnings revisions causing multiple compression, not AI technology becoming useless.

My qualitative concern: significant sector drawdowns are credible; a broad Nasdaq collapse of the 2000–2002 magnitude is a more demanding scenario. This is not a probability forecast.

Market evidence versus portfolio exposure

Market warning signs and personal concentration are deliberately separate. A concentrated portfolio can be unsafe in a healthy market; a diversified portfolio can withstand a severe correction without an all-or-nothing market call.

Decision hierarchy

0. Investigate material news immediately, even between scheduled reviews. 1. Check your allocation limits. 2. Reassess business economics and normalized earnings. 3. Confirm broad weakness through breadth, credit and trend. 4. Decide on a trim, thesis exit or continued hold. 5. Write a re-entry policy before tactical selling.

Monitoring coverage — automatic versus manual

All 22 checklist indicators mapped

Automated = numerical or filing-feed evaluation. Partial = selected data is automatically collected, but a complete judgment requires human review. Manual = no trustworthy complete free feed. A missing feed does NOT check a box as Clear.

Data quality & coverage

Nasdaq trend and drawdowns use the NASDAQ-100 index from FRED, not the QQQM ETF. FRED observations are usually daily closes and can be delayed. VIX and high-yield spreads are also delayed published observations. Finnhub quotes are optional and availability depends on your key. SEC alerts detect new filings, not their economic meaning.

Monthly EPS consensus revisions, holdings look-through, true AI-specific capex ROI, most breadth measures, supplier order quality, weekly RSI and any ticker without permitted history remain manual. Unassessed and stale readings are never presented as safe.

Automation is designed to reduce repetitive checking; it cannot guarantee detection before a fast crash.

How to respond

1. Verify primary source
2. Identify holdings affected
3. Reassess fundamentals and loss budget
4. Record your decision
5. Avoid acting from one headline

Normal conditions: scan trusted market/company headlines and major filings as convenient; review the 22 signals weekly, monthly and quarterly. During unusual volatility: review relevant prices, credit and news at least once each trading day. Unplanned material disclosures require same-day assessment when practicable.

Evidence-based warning checklist · organized by importance and review cadence

22 indicators: 12 primary and 10 supporting. Quarterly describes scheduled financial-statement analysis, not permission to ignore material news until the next quarter. Record observed status and evidence. "Not assessed" is not "Clear". Order: Primary → Supporting, then Weekly → Monthly → Quarterly → Event-driven, then Critical → High → Medium → Low. Earnings × P/E scenario analysis

Simple scenario prices = EPS × P/E; these are not discounted fair values. Keep EPS and P/E definitions consistent (forward vs trailing, GAAP vs adjusted). No dividends or timing modeled.

Historical lesson

In March 2000, the market-weighted P/E for a group of 18 large technology firms exceeded 125× according to Federal Reserve research. That is materially different from many profitable AI leaders today. But even a 25× forward P/E can be risky if the forward EPS estimate is too optimistic.

QQQM daily moving-average monitor

Use 1D chart, 200-session SMA, not 200-week SMA. The conventional 200-day indicator is usually SMA, not EMA. Enter a dated observation; this tool does not fetch market data.

Distance above/below 200-day SMA
Distance above/below 50-day SMA

Distance = (daily close ÷ daily SMA − 1) × 100. Being far above a rising SMA signals an established uptrend but can also mean a late breakdown signal. Compare distance with QQQM's own history, not an arbitrary universal cutoff.
